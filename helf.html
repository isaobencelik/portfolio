The problem

Patients walk out of a consultation with a sheet of instructions, several new medications and more questions than they came with. The system was never built with them in mind, so they fill the gap with Google, online groups and AI chatbots, tools that were never made for it. It is not one bad day. It is every appointment.

Who it's for

Adults between 18 and 65 who manage their own health, and the caregivers who look after someone else's: the son who asks his dad how the surgery went and hears "better, I think". We started in Portugal and Southern Europe, where trust in the system is fragile.

The solution

  • →Find and book verified doctors, filtered by specialty, language and cost
  • →Record the appointment and get a live transcript and summary, with nothing asked of the doctor
  • →An easy-read summary of what the doctor said, plus next steps and medication reminders
  • →One-tap sharing with a caregiver, and a patient-owned health record that is encrypted and never sold

The value

  • →Understanding: every appointment is clear after you leave the room
  • →Continuity: one record that follows you across doctors and countries
  • →Support: family stays involved without intruding
  • →Ownership: the data belongs to the patient, not the system

Key product decisions

Stay on the patient's side

Fitness-first products like Oura and WHOOP track the body. None of them help you inside the healthcare system, where you feel most vulnerable. Helf needs nothing from the doctor, so it can work with any doctor from day one.

Patients pay, institutions scale it

A single B2C subscription at €9.99 a month, with 50% off the first two months and no free tier. Health insurers and employer benefits come later as a B2B channel.

Start where trust is lowest

Lisbon first, then Southern Europe through patient communities and insurer partnerships. The market is large (a global digital health market of around $300B), but trust is what we'd have to earn first.

How we worked

We used design thinking: starting from real patient stories, defining the problem from the patient's side, and prototyping the app as screens in Google Stitch before pitching it. As Product Manager I kept the team pointed at one problem and one user, and turned the findings into the scope you see above. We closed the pitch by asking for the right doors rather than money: strategic mentorship, support from NOVA Marketing Lab, academic partnerships and pilot partners.
